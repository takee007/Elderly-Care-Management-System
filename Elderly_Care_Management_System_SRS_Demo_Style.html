Abstract

The Elderly Care Management System is a Web-based application that automates, streamlines, and centralizes daily eldercare operations and healthcare supervision. Traditionally, elderly care activities—such as medication administration tracking, doctor appointment scheduling, vital sign monitoring, and caregiver shift handoffs—have been managed manually using handwritten paper logs, physical pillboxes, and fragmented telephone communications. Consequently, families and eldercare institutions face significant inefficiencies, frequent medication errors, missed clinical appointments, and potentially fatal delays during sudden medical emergencies.

The Elderly Care Management System directly resolves these critical vulnerabilities by introducing a unified, multi-role digital ecosystem connecting elderly citizens, family guardians, visiting or residential caregivers, and medical practitioners. By offering real-time medication reminders, single-tap adherence logging, digital prescription archives, structured activities of daily living (ADL) checklists, and instantaneous emergency SOS panic alerting with live GPS broadcast, the platform eliminates human error and provides round-the-clock peace of mind for family guardians.

Developed using an accessible frontend architecture and a robust, secure relational backend, the system adheres strictly to health data privacy protocols, role-based access control (RBAC), and universal accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1 AA) tailored for senior citizens. This comprehensive Software Requirements Specification (SRS) report establishes the architectural foundation, functional and non-functional specifications, UML behavioral models, relational database schemas, and verification frameworks governing the development and deployment of the platform.

Introduction

The purpose of the Elderly Care Management System is to automate, digitize, and coordinate home care and health supervision routines for aging seniors. In modern society, shifting socio-demographic structures, the rapid growth of nuclear families, and demanding professional obligations make it increasingly difficult for adult children and family guardians to administer continuous, error-free care for their aging parents manually. The Elderly Care Management System provides an integrated, methodical, web-enabled solution to this pervasive challenge, establishing a consistent and transparent channel for managing geriatric health routines while enhancing safety, speed, accuracy, and overall quality of life.

1.1 Background

Global demographic transitions indicate an unprecedented increase in the senior citizen population. Concurrently, a substantial portion of the elderly demographic experiences chronic conditions such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular ailments, arthritis, and mild cognitive impairments. Managing these multi-morbidities necessitates complex polypharmacy regimens, regular clinical checkups, continuous physiological vital sign tracking, and assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs).

In traditional domestic and institutional care environments, care coordination depends on memory, handwritten paper diaries, and manual telephone inquiries. When family members leave for work or reside in distant cities, they remain in a persistent state of anxiety, unable to verify whether their senior relatives took their critical morning cardiovascular medication, consumed balanced meals, or attended their scheduled clinical consultations. Furthermore, professional caregivers working rotating shifts lack standardized handoff documentation, creating hazardous blind spots in patient history. A centralized web application addresses these challenges by consolidating health data into an auditable, real-time platform accessible from any standard web browser.

1.2 Motivation

Several critical real-world factors motivated the conception and development of the Elderly Care Management System:

  1. Rapid Growth of the Geriatric Demographic: As life expectancy expands and nuclear family arrangements become the predominant societal norm, the reliance on structured digital care management systems has grown exponentially. Families need reliable technological tools that bridge physical distances and ensure proactive elder supervision.
  2. Eradication of Lethal Medication Errors: Medical literature highlights that polypharmacy omissions, dosage duplications, and adverse drug-drug interactions represent leading causes of preventable emergency geriatric hospital admissions. Automating medication schedules with timestamped adherence confirmation removes reliance on vulnerable senior memory.
  3. Elimination of Caregiver Communication Breakdowns: Visiting caregivers, domestic aides, and relatives frequently fail to communicate vital observations during shift changes. A centralized digital handoff log ensures seamless continuity of care, recording completed meals, fluid intake, hygiene, and abnormal vital readings.
  4. Immediate Life-Safety Escalation in Emergencies: In acute episodes such as domestic falls, sudden chest pain, or respiratory distress, seniors often cannot unlock smartphones or dial emergency telephone numbers. A dedicated, one-touch SOS mechanism that dispatches real-time distress alerts with GPS coordinates to both family and paramedics is vital for saving lives.
  5. Actionable Clinical Data for Physicians: Doctors routinely encounter patients who cannot accurately recall their past adherence history or physiological fluctuations. Providing clinicians with verified longitudinal records dramatically improves diagnostic accuracy and therapeutic efficacy.

1.3 Problem Statement

Manual systems, physical paper organizers, and informal verbal communications are still the standard methods employed by families and care aides to manage eldercare routines. These outdated administrative workflows result in severe operational challenges, safety risks, and emotional stress, including:

  1. Medication Mismanagement and Dose Omissions: Elderly individuals frequently forget whether they have consumed their daily prescription doses. Physical pillboxes offer no verification mechanism, leading to dangerous under-dosing or life-threatening accidental double-dosing.
  2. Lack of Real-Time Care Visibility for Family Members: Working guardians lack real-time visibility into the daily wellbeing of their elderly relatives. They must repeatedly disrupt their workdays with phone calls that may go unanswered, causing severe panic and emotional distress.
  3. Unaccountable and Fragmented Caregiver Handoffs: Professional care aides currently record care activities on arbitrary paper notebooks or convey them verbally. If an aide forgets to document a high blood pressure reading or an omitted evening meal, subsequent caregivers remain unaware, compromising patient safety.
  4. Misplaced Prescriptions and Missed Clinic Appointments: Physical paper prescriptions, laboratory blood reports, and doctor appointment slips are easily lost, leading to missed specialist consultations and discontinued therapies.
  5. Delayed Crisis Response During Acute Incidents: When a senior suffers a sudden fall or stroke, every minute delay in emergency response can cause permanent disability or fatality. Current manual setups lack an instantaneous, one-touch panic dispatch mechanism.

The primary objectives of the Elderly Care Management System are:

  1. Centralize Senior Health Baselines: Create an automated, unified cloud repository to maintain complete senior profiles, emergency contacts, primary physicians, chronic conditions, and critical drug allergies without manual paperwork.
  2. Automate Medication Schedules & Adherence Tracking: Provide an intuitive interface to configure dosage timings and enable seniors and caregivers to confirm dose administration with a single click, logging exact timestamps.
  3. Streamline Clinical Appointments & Medical Records: Enable family guardians to schedule, view, and manage doctor consultations, diagnostic tests, and digital prescription scans.
  4. Standardize Daily Care Routines (ADLs) and Shift Handoffs: Provide a structured checklist for daily nutrition, hygiene, mobility, and vital signs, coupled with an immutable shift handoff logging system for caregivers.

System Overview

The Elderly Care Management System allows families, caregivers, and healthcare professionals to coordinate, monitor, and safeguard geriatric health routines efficiently. In the past, eldercare required heavy manual effort, physical paper record-keeping, and disjointed phone calls that introduced dangerous communication gaps. As a result, critical tasks were frequently overlooked, and emergencies escalated unchecked. The Elderly Care Management System addresses these issues by offering an integrated web application that standardizes schedules, logs daily care, and dispatches instant alerts.

2.1 System Users

The system supports five distinct user classes, each with tailored interfaces and granular operational privileges:

  • Elderly Senior Citizen: The primary beneficiary of the platform. Interacts through a high-contrast, large-font interface optimized for seniors with mild visual or motor limitations. Seniors can view daily medication alarms, tap a single large confirmation button to mark doses as 'Taken', review the day's meals, and trigger the high-priority Emergency SOS button during crises.
  • Family Member / Primary Guardian: The administrative overseer of the senior's care plan. Guardians can register senior profiles, input and modify medication regimens, schedule doctor consultations, review caregiver shift notes, monitor adherence analytics, and receive real-time SMS alerts during missed doses or SOS activations.
  • Professional Caregiver: Certified home nurses, visiting aides, or residential care staff. Caregivers access a mobile-optimized shift dashboard to record completed daily living tasks (hygiene, feeding, mobility), log measured physiological vitals (blood pressure, blood glucose, temperature), administer medication doses, and compile digital shift handover reports for incoming staff.
10
  • Healthcare Provider / Doctor: Medical specialists, geriatricians, and clinic physicians overseeing the senior's clinical care. Doctors can securely access authorized patient profiles, review longitudinal medication adherence graphs, examine vital sign trends, formulate electronic prescriptions, and document clinical visit notes directly into the patient's archive.
  • Emergency First Responder / Paramedic: Emergency medical personnel responding to distress alerts. When an SOS alert is activated, first responders receive an automated, time-limited emergency health token granting immediate read-only access to vital baseline data: blood group, known drug allergies (e.g., Penicillin), chronic conditions, home address, and guardian telephone contacts.
  • System Administrator: Technical personnel responsible for maintaining platform integrity, managing user credentials, monitoring database performance, enforcing security policies, inspecting audit trails, and executing automated system backups.

All system interactions are governed by a strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) engine. Each user role is restricted to its designated data domain, ensuring complete confidentiality of personal medical data while maintaining seamless collaboration across the caregiving network.

2.2 System Architecture

The Elderly Care Management System is designed following a modern 3-Tier Layered Architecture, ensuring high maintainability, robust security, scalability, and clean separation of concerns. The architecture comprises three distinct layers:

  1. Presentation Layer (Client Tier): The top-level interface through which users interact with the system via web browsers on desktop computers, tablets, or smartphones. Built using HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript, the presentation tier delivers responsive dashboards tailored to user roles. It features senior-accessible typography (large touch targets, high contrast, ARIA screen-reader labels), interactive adherence charts, and asynchronous AJAX form submissions for smooth user experience.
  2. Application / Business Logic Layer (Server Tier): The middle tier executing core business logic, application routing, and security policies. Developed using PHP 8.2 on an MVC structure, this tier validates inputs, verifies user authentication tokens, coordinates dosage scheduling algorithms, processes caregiver shift handovers, dispatches automated SMS/email alerts via external gateways, and logs all data mutations.
  3. Data & Storage Layer (Database Tier): The persistence layer consisting of a relational MySQL 8.0 database engine. This layer stores user accounts, senior clinical baselines, medication master lists, scheduled dose logs, doctor appointments, daily care checklists, and emergency SOS audit logs. It enforces strict foreign key constraints, indexes high-frequency queries, and secures sensitive medical data.

2.3 System Modules

The Elderly Care Management System is divided into eight well-defined, interoperable modules addressing specific caregiving domains:

Module One: User & Role-Based Authentication: Manages account provisioning, login authentication, password reset workflows, profile customization, and role assignment (Senior, Guardian, Caregiver, Doctor, Admin). It enforces secure session management and access validation across all application endpoints.

Module Two: Elderly Profile & Health Baseline: Maintains comprehensive digital profiles for seniors, documenting full names, birth dates, blood types, home addresses, GPS coordinates, chronic ailments (e.g., hypertension, diabetes), primary physician details, and critical drug allergy flags.

Module Three: Medication Management & Adherence Tracker: Enables guardians and doctors to configure medication regimens, specify dosage units, set daily timings, and attach instructions. Generates visual reminders and allows seniors/caregivers to confirm dose consumption with a single click.

13

Module Four: Doctor Appointment & Clinical History: Streamlines the booking, rescheduling, and tracking of specialist consultations, laboratory diagnostic appointments, and physical therapy sessions. Features digital document uploads for doctor prescriptions, laboratory reports, and discharge summaries.

Module Five: Daily Care Tasks & ADL Routine: Coordinates non-pharmacological activities of daily living (ADLs). Caregivers and guardians establish daily schedules for personal hygiene, meals, fluid intake, and mobility exercises. Caregivers log task completion and record measured physiological vitals (blood pressure, pulse rate, glucose levels).

Module Six: Caregiver Shift Handoff & Care Notes: Standardizes the transition between outgoing and incoming care aides. Outgoing staff must document a structured shift report detailing completed tasks, vital trends, senior mood, and pending instructions, requiring digital acknowledgment by the incoming aide and guardian.

Module Seven: Emergency SOS Alert & Notification: The critical life-safety module. When the senior or caregiver activates the prominent SOS button, the system triggers loud browser audio alarms, sends immediate SMS and email alerts containing GPS coordinates, and generates an emergency medical summary for paramedics.

Module Eight: Family Oversight Dashboard & Reports: Aggregates care data into visual charts, tracking monthly medication compliance percentages, vital sign stability, and daily task completion. Facilitates one-click PDF report generation for clinical review.

2.4 Security and Verification

Given that the Elderly Care Management System processes sensitive personal and clinical data, security is an essential non-negotiable requirement. The system incorporates multi-layered defensive controls:

  1. Authentication: All users must register and authenticate using verified email credentials and strong passwords. User passwords are encrypted using one-way cryptographic hashing via Argon2id / Bcrypt with dynamic salting. Passwords are never stored in plain text.
  2. Authorization & RBAC: Granular Role-Based Access Control ensures that users can only access endpoints and data authorized for their role. Caregivers cannot alter medical prescriptions, seniors cannot access administrative settings, and doctors can only inspect assigned patient records.
  3. Session Management: Authenticated sessions use cryptographically random 128-bit session tokens stored with HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict, and Secure flags to prevent cross-site scripting (XSS) session hijacking. Inactive sessions automatically expire after 20 minutes.
  4. Input Validation & SQL Injection Prevention: All client inputs undergo strict server-side validation and sanitization. All database interactions utilize PHP Data Objects (PDO) with parameterized prepared statements, preventing SQL injection vulnerabilities.
  5. Data Protection & Audit Trail: All communications operate over encrypted HTTPS/TLS 1.3 channels. Critical events—such as medication schedule changes, dose cancellations, and SOS activations—are logged in an immutable audit table with timestamps and user IP addresses.

System Life Cycle

3.1 Software Development Life Cycle

The development of the Elderly Care Management System adheres to a structured Waterfall / Iterative SDLC methodology. Because healthcare software directly impacts patient wellbeing and life safety, a systematic, phased engineering approach is required to guarantee clinical accuracy, requirement stability, and rigorous testing before production deployment.

3.1.1 SDLC Phases

  1. Requirement Analysis: In this opening phase, comprehensive user requirements were elicited through geriatric literature review, family guardian interviews, and clinical safety guidelines. Requirements were classified, refined, and documented in this formal specification.
  2. System Design: Translates verified requirements into detailed technical blueprints. Involves modeling 3-tier architecture, designing normalized relational database schemas (ERD), mapping data flows (DFD), formulating subsystem use cases, and creating accessible wireframes.
  3. Implementation (Coding): Modular construction of frontend interfaces (HTML5/CSS3/JS) and backend domain controllers (PHP 8.2, MySQL). Core modules—Authentication, Medication, Appointments, Care Routines, and Emergency SOS—are programmed following PSR-12 coding conventions.
  4. Testing & Quality Assurance: Rigorous validation including unit tests, integration tests across database boundaries, usability testing with senior personas, security vulnerability audits, and latency simulations for the SOS dispatch engine.
  5. Deployment & Release: Deploying the verified application onto a production cloud server environment configured with HTTPS/TLS certificates, database indexing, and automated backup cron jobs.
  6. Maintenance & Evolution: Post-release monitoring, server performance tuning, periodic security patch installation, and ongoing feature refinements based on stakeholder feedback.

Requirement Engineering

Requirement Engineering ensures that system requirements are systematically gathered, analyzed, refined, resolved of ambiguities, and documented with absolute clarity.

4.1 Requirement Elicitation

During the elicitation phase, multi-source techniques were employed:

  • Stakeholder Interviews: Conducted in-depth interviews with family guardians managing eldercare and professional home caregivers to identify everyday pain points, forgotten tasks, and emergency vulnerabilities.
  • Domain Documentation Review: Reviewed geriatric clinical guidelines, traditional paper medication charts, and caregiver handoff logs to understand the essential data entities and regulatory standards.
  • Direct Observation: Observed typical daily routines of senior citizens, noting visual, cognitive, and physical interaction constraints that dictate accessibility design.

4.2 Requirement Analysis

4.2.1 Feasibility Study

A rigorous feasibility assessment was conducted across four primary dimensions:

Technical Feasibility: The technology stack (PHP, MySQL, HTML5/CSS3, JavaScript, Twilio API) consists of proven, open-source technologies capable of sub-second query execution, low memory overhead, and responsive rendering on all modern web browsers without specialized hardware.

Economic Feasibility: The platform utilizes open-source development frameworks and database engines, avoiding costly licensing fees. Operational cloud hosting expenses are minimal, while the return on investment (ROI)—measured in preventable emergency medical hospitalizations—is substantial.

Operational Feasibility: The interface incorporates large typography, high contrast, and simplified single-click workflows specifically tailored for non-technical seniors and busy caregivers, ensuring rapid adoption and low training barriers.

20

Schedule Feasibility: The project scope has been structured into modular sprints executable within the academic semester timeframe (12 weeks / 48 working days), ensuring timely delivery of all documentation, database models, and functional modules.

4.2.2 Requirement Classification

All gathered requirements were systematically classified into three distinct categories:

  1. Functional Requirements: Core capabilities including user authentication, senior profile management, medication scheduling, adherence logging, clinic appointments, daily care checklists, caregiver shift handoffs, and emergency SOS dispatch.
  2. Non-Functional Requirements: Operational constraints and quality attributes including performance (page load < 2s), security (Argon2id hashing, TLS 1.3), reliability (99.9% uptime), accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA), and data integrity.
  3. User Interface Requirements: Ergonomic design criteria including high-contrast color palettes, 18px+ font options, prominent touch targets, and responsive cross-device layouts.

4.2.3 Conflict and Redundancy Resolution

During stakeholder elicitation, several operational conflicts were identified and resolved:

  • Conflict: Caregivers requested rapid single-click shift logging, whereas Guardians demanded granular vital records. Resolution: Implemented smart pre-populated forms with mandatory vital fields and optional detailed notes.
  • Conflict: Modification of medication schedules. Resolution: Restricted prescription schedule alterations exclusively to Guardians and Doctors; caregivers are restricted to logging administration events with explanatory rationale.

Non-Functional Requirements

The non-functional requirements define the quality attributes, operational constraints, and performance benchmarks governing the Elderly Care Management System. They ensure the platform remains reliable, secure, accessible, and scalable over long-term usage.

7.1 Feature List with Description

  1. Performance & Response Latency:
    • Standard page loads, dashboard queries, and schedule lookups must render in under 2.0 seconds over standard 4G and domestic broadband connections.
    • Database query execution latency must remain under 200 milliseconds for 95% of transactions.
    • Critical Emergency SOS dispatch routines must execute within 1.0 second of trigger confirmation, ensuring zero latency during life-safety crises.
  2. Reliability & Availability:
    • The system must maintain 99.9% scheduled uptime throughout production deployment.
    • Automated nightly database snapshots must be stored in redundant, offsite storage to prevent data loss in the event of server failures.
    • Graceful degradation ensures that if external SMS gateways experience latency, local audio-visual alarms continue operating without failure.
  3. Security & Health Data Confidentiality:
    • All HTTP transactions must strictly operate over Transport Layer Security (TLS 1.3) with valid SSL certificates.
    • All stored user passwords must use salted cryptographic hashing via Argon2id / Bcrypt with cost factor 12.
    • Sensitive senior baseline data—such as chronic conditions and drug allergies—is encrypted at rest using AES-256 standards.
  4. Usability & Senior Accessibility:
    • The interface strictly complies with WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards, featuring high-contrast themes and scalable fonts (18px+ default for seniors).
    • Interactive buttons feature large touch targets (minimum 48x48 pixels) to accommodate elderly users with mild motor tremors or arthritis.
49
  1. Scalability:
    • The system architecture utilizes a stateless PHP application tier capable of horizontal scaling behind standard load balancers.
    • The MySQL database schema incorporates indexed lookup keys on high-frequency tables (Dose Logs, Appointments, Vitals), supporting up to 10,000 active senior care plans simultaneously without database locking contention.
  2. Portability & Cross-Browser Compatibility:
    • The web application is fully responsive and verified to execute seamlessly across all major modern browsers: Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Apple Safari, and Microsoft Edge.
    • Mobile responsiveness is guaranteed on iOS and Android smartphones and tablets without requiring proprietary native downloads.
  3. Maintainability & Code Standards:
    • All backend source code adheres to PSR-12 coding style guidelines, featuring modular MVC separation and comprehensive docstring documentation.
    • Database migrations and seeders are version-controlled to enable rapid deployment and consistent staging across testing and production environments.
  4. Data Integrity & Auditability:
    • All database tables enforce strict relational foreign key constraints, preventing orphaned records.
    • Critical medical events—including medication additions, dose cancellations, and SOS activations—are committed to an append-only audit trail with immutable timestamps and IP records.

Conclusion

This Software Requirements Specification proposes a comprehensive, methodical, and human-centric approach to modernizing and automating geriatric care routines. Traditional eldercare systems—heavily reliant on physical pillboxes, handwritten notebooks, and fragmented phone communications—expose aging seniors to preventable medication omissions, shift communication breakdowns, and fatal emergency response delays.

The proposed Elderly Care Management System addresses these vulnerabilities through an integrated, multi-role web platform. By synchronizing medication schedules with real-time adherence logging, streamlining doctor consultations and digital prescription archives, standardizing daily living (ADL) routines, enforcing caregiver shift handoff accountability, and providing an instantaneous one-touch Emergency SOS broadcast with GPS coordinates and medical briefs, the platform establishes an unbroken circle of care around the senior.

The architectural design, relational schema, UML behavioral models, and verification frameworks articulated throughout this report demonstrate that the system is technically, economically, and operationally feasible. Built upon an accessible frontend and a secure relational backend, the Elderly Care Management System delivers profound socio-technological value: eliminating dangerous medication errors, fostering professional accountability, and granting working family members 24/7 peace of mind while preserving the independence and dignity of aging senior citizens.
